Rahul Shah is a product designer who designs the moment someone starts learning, and what keeps them going. Since 2021 he has designed Encourage, a college and career readiness platform that reaches 1 million students in K–12 education (Wikipedia, opens in new tab): the onboarding (Wikipedia, opens in new tab) students start in, the tools their school counselors (Wikipedia, opens in new tab) use, and the design system (Wikipedia, opens in new tab) under both.

His work starts with research and ends with a measured result, and he designs to WCAG (Wikipedia, opens in new tab) 2.1 AA from the first sketch. He studied human-centered computing (Wikipedia, opens in new tab) at RIT and learning design (Wikipedia, opens in new tab) at UMGC, and in his own time built Chiron, an AI tutor (Wikipedia, opens in new tab) that runs as a Claude (Wikipedia, opens in new tab) Skill.

Encourage onboarding: twice the questions, and students still finish

Encoura, research then an experiment. Status: shipping Fall 2026.

Our classroom research found students weren't stalling on the questions. They stalled on account verification and blocked school inboxes. So when the survey had to double, I rebuilt the multi-step flow (Wikipedia, opens in new tab) around that: chunked (Wikipedia, opens in new tab) by topic to cut cognitive load (Wikipedia, opens in new tab), each chunk with its own short progress bar (Wikipedia, opens in new tab), and a way back from a mistyped or blocked email. In Encoura's first in-app A/B test (Wikipedia, opens in new tab), completion held near 84% with twice the questions.

Chiron: an AI tutor that gives you exactly what you need

Independent project, a Claude Skill. Source: Chiron on GitHub (opens in new tab).

My own project, built as a Claude (Wikipedia, opens in new tab) Skill. Chiron is a tutor in the Socratic (Wikipedia, opens in new tab) tradition: it asks questions until it finds what you can't yet explain, builds a lesson in that gap, your zone of proximal development (Wikipedia, opens in new tab), and adapts the next one to your answers. Its checks are formative (Wikipedia, opens in new tab): each wrong option stands for a specific misconception (Wikipedia, opens in new tab), and the course ends on flashcards (Wikipedia, opens in new tab) you write yourself. The design problem was giving each learner exactly what they need in that moment, no more and no less.

Encourage for Educators: hundreds of students, one clear next step

Encoura, staff tools. Status: shipped.

The staff side of Encourage. Counselors (Wikipedia, opens in new tab) track hundreds of students at once, so I built groups, rosters and status tracking around how they already organize their day: filters and search (Wikipedia, opens in new tab) for finding students, and an empty state that puts one clear call to action (Wikipedia, opens in new tab) in front of them.
