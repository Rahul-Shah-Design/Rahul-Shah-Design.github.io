# Critique: W1 — Why a sourdough starter rises, and why it dies

## Top finding
The plan is required to contain **every** gradable check with the misconception each wrong
option encodes (#18). For Module 1 and Module 3 it does. For Module 2 — the only module
actually built — it does not: the plan replaces all of them with a promissory note.
"**Module 2, gate after §2 (tests §1):** written in full in the module below." /
"**Module 2, gate after §4 (tests §3):** written in full in the module below." /
"**Module 2, two instrument checks and a 5-item end-of-module set:** written in full below."
That is a placeholder standing where the contract asks for content, and it is not excused by
the harness scoping the output to module 2 — the plan is written *before* the module and is
the thing that is supposed to constrain it. The cost shows up immediately and exactly where
the plan stopped thinking. The plan said the gate after §2 would test §1. The delivered gate
after §2 tests §2, using §2's own worked example verbatim: §2 opens "Into jar A you stir one
tablespoon of ripe starter with a cup of fresh flour and water. Into jar B you stir four
tablespoons into the same amount of fresh flour and water. **Jar B domes first** — well before
jar A," and the gate seven paragraphs later asks "Into jar B you stir **four** tablespoons
with the same cup of fresh flour and water. Which one domes first?" The answer is printed
above the question. This violates #28 ("Never place an item directly beneath the section that
answered it. Lag by at least one section") in its purest form and turns the module's only
mid-module gate into a reading-comprehension check on the paragraph the learner just read.

## Ranked findings

1. **The interleaved items are the module-1 items, not reformulations of them** — checklist
   #29, and the named failure mode "repeated (not reformulated) interleaved item." The plan's
   Module 1 end-of-module set already contains "Numeric with tolerance — *A jar weighs 480 g at
   feeding and doubles in volume. Weight at peak, ±5 g?* 480 (a gram or two under)." Module 2's
   item 4 is that item: "right after feeding: **480 g**… Six hours later it has risen to a bit
   over twice its height… **About 480 g, maybe a gram or two under** — **Correct.**" Same jar,
   same 480 g, same doubling, same answer. Item 3 is likewise the Module 1 gate — plan: "*Order
   these: water wets flour · enzymes cut starch into sugar · a tenant absorbs the sugar · gas is
   released*" — with two steps appended and the same named misconception ("this is the most
   common way to get this wrong, and it comes from treating flour as food the moment it goes
   in"). Both are the earlier item with the surface unchanged, which is the failure mode the
   rule exists to prevent: the learner re-recognises a remembered item instead of retrieving the
   discrimination in a new dress. Worse, the plan asserts the opposite in writing: "Both
   reformulated: M1 delivered them as prose about flour and gluten; they come back as a sequence
   to order and as a kitchen scale reading." The plan's own Module 1 section shows they came as
   a sequence to order and a kitchen scale reading the first time too.

2. **The peak clock's readouts contradict the arithmetic the module teaches, and that flips a
   graded answer** — #30 (instrument carries a claim), #24 (real computed output), #27
   (per-option explanations must be true). The module hands the learner a rule and a number:
   "The time it takes the crowd to double is the **doubling time**, and for the yeast in a
   counter-top starter it is roughly two and a half hours in a warm kitchen," and then uses it
   as the explanation for a distractor: "1.6 doublings at two and a half hours each is four
   hours, not seven." Apply the same rule to the other dial positions and the instrument breaks.
   1:5:5 needs 3.46 doublings; at two and a half hours each that is 8.6 hours — past seven — yet
   the check marks it wrong: "**1:5:5** — *6.2 hours… you are still forty minutes short*," and
   awards the point to 1:10:10. A learner who uses the rule the module just told them to hold on
   to gets graded wrong for it. The same incoherence runs through the §2 gate ("at roughly two
   and a half hours a doubling that is about five hours saved" for two doublings, i.e. 2.5 h per
   doubling) while the dial spends roughly 1.1 h per doubling, and through end-of-module item 2's
   explanation: "*Around 7.5:* that is 62 °F with a 1:1:1 feed. The bigger feed adds about two
   extra doublings and each one is slower in a cold room" — two extra doublings at the stated
   cold doubling time of 4.8 hours is nine and a half hours, which would put the answer near 17,
   not the 11.5 the item requires. The figures themselves are internally consistent and honestly
   sourced; the instrument readouts are not from the same model, and the mismatch lands on the
   one quantity this learner's whole outcome depends on — how big a feed is big enough.

3. **The boundary adaptation is announced and then not carried out** — #32. The plan-update
   block promises: "M2-C3 promoted to the module's spine and given the \"flat top means the
   yeast died\" error as a named distractor in the gate after §4 **and in end-of-module item
   1**." The gate after §4 delivers it. End-of-module item 1 does not: its stem is "the jar peaks
   in three hours instead of the usual six," and its three distractors encode maturity, old
   flour, and a cold kitchen. The learner's actual stated misconception — the one they wrote
   twice ("the flat top means the yeast in B is dead or dying," "wouldn't just adding more flour
   fix it") — is tested once, in a gate, and never returns in the graded set that is supposed to
   be the module's exit. A boundary that is only half-honoured is half a page break.

4. **Peak height is taught as fixed and then quietly isn't** — #6 ("what to notice"), #24. §2:
   "both jars top out at about the same height." Thermostat: "the peak height readout still says
   **2.2×**. It did not move… Sixteen degrees Fahrenheit nearly doubled the time to peak and
   changed the peak height by nothing at all." Then Figure 3, on the learner's own routine,
   peaks at "**1:20 pm** | 76 °F | **2.34× ← highest**" — higher than any other curve in the
   module, on the smallest feed on the dial, and nothing says why. The learner's only actual
   measuring device is a rubber band on the glass. The module spends two sections telling them
   height carries no information about crowd or ratio or temperature, then shows them a height
   that moved, and leaves them without a rule for reading the one thing they can see.

5. **Two open asks in one turn** — named failure mode "two asks in one message." The boundary
   turn ends "One from module 1 first, in your own words, no looking back: **flour goes in the
   jar dry. Something has to happen to it before any tenant can eat it. What?**" and then, in
   the same turn, ships module 2 with "**Delivery message:** Module 2 is the nine hours between
   your 2 pm jar and your 11 pm jar. The question to bring back here is in the block at the end."
   The learner is holding a free-recall question and a module whose handoff sets a second
   question, with no ordering between them. The retrieval ask is the one that gets dropped.

6. **Modules 1 and 3 exist only as plan bullets** — #22, #26, #31. "> *[Module 1 artifact built
   and delivered in this same turn. Its contents are not reproduced here; the output spec asks
   for module 2 in full.]*" The harness scoped this, so it is not a dodge — but it means the
   contract is only auditable on one module, and the two modules carrying the payload claim
   (T12, "their two starters were almost certainly never dead") and the whole canonical floor
   for M1 and M3 are untested prose. Every claim in the plan about M1 and M3 — including that
   M3-C4 "ships as a labelled table plus the drag-to-label item" — is an assertion about writing
   that does not exist here.

## Hollow compliance

- The interleaving schedule satisfies the counting rule and nothing else: "**Module 2's
  end-of-module set pulls forward:** … Both reformulated… **2 of 5 items.**" Two of five clears
  the one-third bar (#29) on the page while both items are lifts from Module 1's own set. The
  arithmetic is honoured; the discrimination the rule protects is not.
- The peak clock's per-option feedback reads as reasoned but argues against itself two lines
  apart: "**1:2:2** — *Closer, but adding one more part of flour has bought you less than an
  hour. **Each extra doubling costs the same time**, and doublings go up slowly as flour goes up
  fast.*" If each doubling costs the same time, and the module says that time is two and a half
  hours, then 1:1:1 → 1:2:2 (+0.7 doublings) costs nearly two hours, not "less than an hour." The
  explanation is confidently phrased around a rule the numbers beside it break.
- The fold-back line is printed twice, identically — once as the module's closing sentence and
  again as the first line of the handoff block ("**Your jar rises because a crowd doubles toward
  a ceiling the flour sets, and it falls because the acid softens the web faster than the yeast
  stops filling it.**"). #31 asks the handoff to open with the fold-back; #5 asks for anything
  not serving the claim to be cut. Satisfying the first by copy-paste means the module ends on
  the same sentence twice with five questions wedged between them.
- "**Module 3, instrument check (the dilution dial):** *Find the smallest feed ratio that holds
  the jar below pH 3.8 for five straight days at 70 °F.* Set-the-control-until-it-flips. Around
  1:4:4." This is a planned check written as a target answer with no options and no
  misconceptions, in a plan whose stated job (#18) is to record the misconception behind each
  wrong option. It looks like a planned gradable item; there is nothing gradable specified.
